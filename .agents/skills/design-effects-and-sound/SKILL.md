# Finish the approved picture

Bind the passes and any cue sheet to a named graphic and selected picture version. Save the editable finishing layers or timeline, referenced stems, destination preview and requested encoded mix together. Recheck timing after picture changes.

Name the event in a verb and a moment: gathering charge, shedding sparks, rotating, arriving, settling. For each effect identify the emitter, direction, occluder, receiver and what must stay legible. Keep the approved bare artwork available for comparison. For a family of assets, compare the perceived strength across the set in their actual masks. Identical particle positions can look much weaker against a brighter sky.

**Construct the passes separately.** Put the core or origin on the emitting surface. Let a few secondary shapes describe the path, varying scale, spacing, brightness and termination. Mask a branch behind a foreground element and let it emerge only where geometry permits. Give contact or impact its own accent. Put reflected light on facing planes, with falloff and enough underlying texture to read the material. Preserve a quieter area around required copy. Give each moving object its own adjustable wake or blur, aligned to its travel, outlet or hub axis.

Toggle each pass and compare the full assembly to the clean parent at delivery size. If “more impressive” makes the main plume too large, restore the chosen silhouette and work on hot ember heads, short wakes and spill. If a glow looks pasted on, inspect the receiver mask and existing light before increasing its opacity. Test that removing the new group returns the approved base when that is the revision contract.

**Refit dependent effects after replacing a subject.** Hide the subject and solo the reflection, matte, shadow and bloom in turn. A duplicate fingertip or old product edge can live inside a lighting layer. Rebuild that receiver mask against the new silhouette. In an effects-off crop mark the intended emitter point or patch; in some designs that is deliberately offset. In the registered effects-on crop locate the first bright core, separately from its surrounding glow. Check every emitter in each requested layout. Preserve the approved visual character of the effect while correcting its origin, direction and contact.

## When the deliverable has time and sound

1. Write the duration, frame rate, reveal sequence, dialogue and loop or end-card requirement. Block the sequence silently. If a supplied song sets the edit, mark its phrases early and refine the mix after picture timing settles. Choose a track or stem with permission for the intended use. Record the source and permitted delivery with the project.
2. Mark a short cue sheet with timecode, visible event, musical landmark, sound effect and tail. Choose a phrase change for the main reveal. Leave some actions silent so that the strongest accent has room. For a six-second product reveal, one workable study is 0–2 s approach over a quiet intro, 2–4 s light sweep with a short rising texture, 4 s product lockup on a downbeat, and 4–6 s readable hold with a decaying tail. These are example timings.
3. Keep music, dialogue and spot effects on separate tracks. Trim at phrase boundaries, slip or trim the cue to the visible contact frame, and fade cuts or crossfade a loop. Shape level automation around speech and the important transient. Check the combined peak and loudness against the delivery specification, leaving room for the encoded version.
4. Play the **exported** file from start to finish with sound, then muted at target size. Check first and last frames, cue-to-picture sync, dialogue intelligibility, clipping, cut tails and the loop seam on consecutive plays where relevant. Listen on a small speaker or mono fold-down if that is how the audience hears it. Identify any playback condition you could not test.

For a short result sting, check the last frame before the reveal and the first frame after it. A winner’s accent color can give away the result before the score or gong arrives. Read the decoded audio onset against that cut and inspect the tail before the file ends. Keep the dry cue available on its own. Record measured timing, peak and listening observations separately. A decoder can establish synchronization even on a host where the reviewer cannot hear the timbre.

Deliver the editable timeline, cue sheet, media references and requested playable mix or stems. For a still-image brief stop at the visual proof. The music procedure extends the studio's documented visual workflow to timed work.
